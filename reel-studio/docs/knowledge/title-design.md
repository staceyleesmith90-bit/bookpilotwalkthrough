# Title design: what makes CapCut-style titles look expensive

Studied from popular vlog/lifestyle title templates (e.g. "A Day With Me", "Mini Vlog",
"Life Update", "Today Activity", "Slice of Weekend", "TODAY vlog"). These are the *patterns*;
our templates are original implementations of them.

## The six patterns
1. **Script over serif.** A flowing script word (Pinyon, Great Vibes, Allura) crossing or
   tucked under a classic serif (Bodoni, Playfair, Cormorant). The contrast of formal + human
   is the whole look.
2. **Tiny tracked kicker.** A small uppercase label above the title ("MINI VLOG", "VISUAL
   DIARY") with very wide letter-spacing (0.12–0.3em). It frames the title like a magazine.
3. **A detail line.** Date ("January 2026"), location with a pin, or episode tag ("Eps #1").
   Small, quiet, makes it feel like a series or a diary entry.
4. **A shape.** Oval outline badge, pill tag with a dot, thin rule under a place name.
   One shape max.
5. **Sparkle accents.** 2–3 stars/sparkles around a playful title, in the pop colour with a
   white sticker outline, slightly rotated, gently floating.
6. **Depth.** The big word sits behind the person's head; or a soft shadow lifts white text off
   bright footage.

## Type rules
- Colour: white or cream on footage (soft shadow ~45%), the brand's pop colour for ONE word.
- Script sizes are big (2–3× the kicker). Serif caps mid. Kicker smallest.
- Line spacing of stacked script lines is tight (they may touch).
- Keep titles in the upper-middle third on talking heads (face stays clear) unless behind.
- 2–5 words. Long sentences belong in captions or a `typed` title.

## Motion
- Kicker: fade-up or tracking-in (letters settle).
- Script: write-on (left → right reveal with a feathered edge) — feels hand-lettered.
- Serif caps: slow scale-down 112% → 100% with fade.
- Shapes/sparkles: pop with overshoot, then float.
- Stagger layers 0.15–0.5s. Exit together with a quick fade.
- Sound: a soft swish per write-on, a key click per typed character, a gentle hit or riser for
  bold openers. Keep volume low (titles are decoration, the voice is king).

## Pairing titles with the rest of the reel
- The title's script font = the brand's accent feel; stickers and badges use the brand's main
  font; captions use the caption font. Three voices, one brand.
- Match stickers to the title style: sparkle title → star/sparkle stickers; editorial title →
  thin doodles or none; episode title → brush arrows and circles.
