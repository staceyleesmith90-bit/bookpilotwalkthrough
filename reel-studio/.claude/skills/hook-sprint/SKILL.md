# Hook sprint

First read `.claude/skills/script-kit-library/house-rules.md` and follow it the whole way through. Then read
`.claude/skills/script-kit-library/method.md` (the three jobs, the three hook layers, the pre-ship check).

Wording doesn't predict performance. So write ten, keep the best, and test the top two.

## 0. Know who it's for

If `script-desk/about-me.md` doesn't exist and `brand-kit.md` at the engine root is missing or still contains a
double angle bracket (`<<`), you don't know who they're writing for yet. Don't guess and don't stop. Ask one
question: "Quick one first: who are your reels for? One line is fine, like 'new moms going back to work'. If you
sell a product, tell me who buys it." Use their answer as the audience for this sprint (the customer, for a
product seller), then carry on. Remember the tip in step 7.

## 1. Get the idea

- **"hook sprint: [an idea]":** use the idea.
- **A bracket that points at their desk**, like "[the same idea]" or "the same idea as last time": use the idea
  from their most recent hook sprint (the newest script file with a `## Hook sprint`) and say which one.
- **An idea on their desk**, like "one of my ideas" or "the grow idea you just added": find it in
  `script-desk/ideas.md`. If one line clearly matches, use it and say which. If two or more could, list them, one
  line each, and ask which.
- **Just "hook sprint", or the lesson's placeholder left unfilled**, like "[your idea]": help them find one. Read
  their topics and moments (`script-desk/about-me.md`, or `brand-kit.md` if that doesn't exist yet) and
  `script-desk/ideas.md`. Offer three ideas, one line each, each tagged with a job, then "or tell me yours". Add
  all three to `script-desk/ideas.md` (`from: hook sprint (suggested)`), then wait for their pick.

## 2. Read

- `script-desk/about-me.md` and `script-desk/voice-dna.md`. If they don't exist yet, use the audience and voice
  notes in `brand-kit.md`, and remember the tip in step 7.
- `script-desk/reels-i-like.md` if it exists: the patterns they're drawn to get a slight edge.
- `.claude/skills/script-kit-library/hook-library.md` and `levers.md`. On a second sprint of the same idea, or
  when the job's fifteen don't fit, also `.claude/skills/script-kit-library/more-hooks.md`, the bonus set, so the
  new ten don't repeat the old ten's patterns.

## 3. Pick the job and the kind of reel

If they didn't say, suggest the job for this idea (reach, grow or convert) in one line, and keep going with your
suggestion. They can change it after. For someone just starting out with no offer, suggest reach or grow.

The job decides what a hook is:
- **A talking reel** (most grow and convert reels): each hook is the spoken first line, the "Said" layer. Its
  first sentence is under 12 words.
- **A reach reel over b-roll**: each hook is the on-screen hook block, the "Read" layer, with no voice. Write the
  who line once (it can stay the same across the ten), then ten big lines, each read in a second or two.

## 4. Write ten hooks

- Build each on a pattern marked "opener" that fits the job, from the Hook Library or the bonus set. Most come
  from that job's patterns. One or two strong ones can come from another job. Proof beats and asks never go in a
  hook.
- Write fresh from their moments and their people's words. Never reuse a Hook Library line, Meg's or a
  translation, even in the same niche.
- Something concrete in the first sentence (a time, a place, an object, an exact number or a named moment). A loop opener can leave it
  to the first frame. No keyword or ask, and no number about themselves in the first sentence.
- First person only for what's theirs (see "True stories only" in the house rules). When the idea's story isn't
  theirs yet, write the hooks in "you", the viewer's moment.
- Vary the openings. No first word more than twice across the ten ("Nobody", "Why", "POV").
- Vary the shapes. No more than two hooks share a structure (an "it's not X, it's Y", a "Why does nobody", a
  "Here's why" ending). Ten rewordings of one thought is one hook, not ten.
- Vary the angle. Cover at least four sides of the idea across the ten: their moment, what it costs, the flip (what
  turned out to be true), and the viewer's side of it.
- Don't borrow another reel's whole idea. A line that is the heart of another script file, or of a reel in this
  week's plan, stays with that reel.
- Spread the levers: at least six different levers across the ten. Stack two or three where it comes naturally.
- Use only their facts. Leave a bracket for any number or result they haven't given you.
- Run the pre-ship check on every hook before you show it. Fix anything that fails, quietly.

## 5. Pick two to test

- Mark the two strongest for this job. For each: the lever that carries it, and why, in one line.
- The test: the one thing that differs between the two, so a trial reel can tell them which worked.
- For the top pick, the three layers: said (the hook, or "no voice" on a b-roll reach reel), shown (the first
  frame), read (the hook block from `method.md`: a top line that names who it's for, and a big line that does a
  different job than the spoken line; on a b-roll reach reel, the hook block is the whole hook).

## 6. Save

- **A new idea:** start `script-desk/scripts/YYYY-MM-DD-short-title.md` from
  `.claude/skills/script-kit-library/templates/film-ready-script-template.md`. Fill in the title, the Job on the
  top line (leave register, format and length for `script this` unless the idea makes them obvious), the Idea
  line, `## Hook sprint`, and `## Hook` (the top pick's three layers).
- **An idea that already has a script file:** add the new sprint under `## Hook sprint` as "Sprint 2" (3, 4...)
  with today's date, and keep the old ones. A file with a hook but no sprint yet: keep that hook as "Sprint 1 ·
  the starting hook". Move the old top pick's layers into its own sprint block, and put the
  new top pick in `## Hook`.
- Add the idea to `script-desk/ideas.md` with its job tag and the script path, if it isn't there yet.

## 7. Reply

Keep it easy to scan:

1. First line: "Ten hooks for: [idea] ([job])"
2. The ten, numbered. Under each hook, its pattern and lever(s) in one short line.
3. "Test these two:" the picks, each with its one line of why, then the one thing to change between them.
4. The three layers for the top pick.
5. "Saved to your script desk: script-desk/scripts/[file]"
   When the hooks are in "you" because the story isn't theirs yet, add one line: "These are in 'you' for now. If
   this happened to you, tell me what really happened and I'll make them yours."
6. At most one tip, for the missing file that would sharpen this most: you asked who it's for in step 0 → "Tip:
   finish your brand kit in the engine lesson Make it your own, then say `set up my script desk`, and your next
   hooks get sharper." No `about-me.md` yet → "Tip: say `set up my script desk` and your next hooks get sharper." Otherwise, no `voice-dna.md` → "Tip: say `build my voice DNA`
   and they'll sound even more like you."
7. Last line: "Next: film the top two, or say `script this` to write the whole reel."
