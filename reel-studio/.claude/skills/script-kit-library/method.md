# The method

The rules every Script Kit skill scripts by. Each one is taught in the course. When a skill explains a choice,
it names the rule from here in plain words.

## The five principles

1. **Seen first.** People stop when they recognize their own moment (a time of day, a room, a thought they've
   had but never said), not when they're handed information. Name the moment, never a category of person.
2. **One reel, one job.** Reach new people, grow the ones who found you, or convert them to act. The job decides
   the length, the register, whether it resolves, the ask, and how you judge it.
3. **Your voice can't be copied.** Claude writes the structure. The concept, the phrases and the proof stay
   theirs. Steal the shape, never the story.
4. **The reel is its own demo.** Script what people see, so the reel proves its own point.
5. **Write ten, keep one.** Wording doesn't predict performance. Write a spread, test, and let the numbers pick.

## The three jobs

| Job | What it's for | Usually | Judge it by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | new people finding you | short on-screen lines over b-roll, 5 to 10 seconds | plays against your median for that format |
| Grow | turning viewers into your people | talking, names a tension, 45 to 100 seconds | shares, saves and follows |
| Convert | getting people to act | teaching that pays off, then one ask | comments per 1,000 plays on the keyword |

Judge a reel by the wrong number and the wrong reel wins. A reel with fewer views can still be the one that
brought the followers.

## The two registers

- **Teaching** pays off: a lesson, a list or a result. It gets the full edit.
- **Confessional** names a feeling and never fixes it. No lesson, no hopeful turn at the end. Bolt a fix onto a
  confessional and the contract breaks. It gets a lean edit.

A grow reel is confessional unless its story pays off. A story with a payoff (the first week it all worked) is
told in the teaching register, and its payoff is the proof.

## Who the reel is for

Every hook and script speaks to the person in "Who it's for" in `script-desk/about-me.md`. The kind of account
decides who that person is:

- **Teaches or serves** (coaching, a service, a course, a community): the person she helps, in the situation that
  makes them need her. Most examples in this kit are this kind.
- **Sells a product** (candles, clothes, printables, a shop): the customer, in the moment they'd want it. Maker
  stories and behind-the-scenes reels are welcome, but they're told for the customer: the craft is the proof and
  the story, never a lesson for other makers. The hook block names the customer ("people who light a candle every
  night"), never "makers" or "small shops". Write to other makers only when she says they're who she's after.
- **A creator with nothing of her own to sell** (her life, her style, her home, her family, what she loves; maybe
  brand deals or affiliate links): the follower she's building, and what they come to her for. A laugh, a
  feeling, ideas, a life they like watching. There's often no problem to solve, so the tension is a feeling, a
  question or a small truth they recognize. Her asks are follow, save, send and comment. A brand deal or an
  affiliate link is her offer when she has one, and a convert reel shows it in her real life before any ask.

When the kind isn't on the desk yet, read it from her offer, her brand kit and her reels. Still unclear: ask once.

## Seen, not diagnosed

Naming a hard thing you and the viewer both live inside is solidarity. Implying they're late, lazy or failing is
a diagnosis, and people scroll away. Test every line: does the viewer feel seen, or diagnosed?

## The three hook layers

A hook is three layers pointed at one question:
- **Said:** the spoken first line. Its first sentence is under 12 words; the pattern can run two or three short
  sentences.
- **Shown:** what the first frame shows in the first second.
- **Read:** the hook block, the one block of text at the top of the frame. Usually two short lines: a small top
  line that names who it's for and their situation ("night shift moms", "moms who can't turn it off"), and a
  bigger line that names the tension and opens a loop. One line can do both when it's short. It never repeats the
  spoken line, never runs in all caps, and on a confessional it never hints at a happy ending. On a b-roll reel
  with no voice, the hook block is the whole hook: the same two lines, read in a few seconds.

Write it top line first: `Read: night shift moms / goodnight at breakfast`. The bottom line is the big one. If the
big line should come first, mark it: `Read: goodnight at breakfast (big) / night shift moms`. A feeling with no one
in it catches no one, so the who is never optional.

## The pre-ship check (every hook)

1. Something concrete lands first: a time, a place, an object, an exact number or a named moment, in the first sentence or
   in the first frame. A category of people on its own doesn't count.
2. No brag or result number about yourself in sentence one. A number from the moment itself (a time, a count, a
   price) is fine. Proof goes in the middle.
3. The hook block does a different job than the spoken line, and one of its lines names who it's for: for a
   product seller, the customer, never other makers.
4. The hook block's big line opens a loop or names a tension. A time or a place on its own doesn't.
5. No keyword or ask in the hook, and no warm-up like "I'm about to" or "Let me show you". A "Here's why", "Here's
   how" or "Here's what happened" tail is optional in every pattern: drop it when the line before already opens
   the loop, and never end more than two of ten hooks on one.
6. No AI tells, and no all caps on screen.
7. It sounds like them (their Voice DNA).

These match the engine's own checklist for hooks, so a hook that passes here passes the edit too.

## Shapes and their beats

Beats come from the shape, not a one-size template.

| Shape | Beats |
|---|---|
| Story | setup, trigger, turn, result |
| List | promise, items, payoff |
| Contrarian take | claim, why it's wrong, receipt, reframe |
| Confessional | the unsaid thing, the tension, the question left open |
| Comparison | this, that, the part nobody sees in the middle |
| Series episode | where we left off, the new piece, what's next |
| One line (b-roll) | the scene, the line, the beat it lands on |

**The "but and so" test:** join the beats with "but" and "so". If they only join with "and then", it's a list:
fine for a tutorial, flat for a story.

## Retention is written, not edited in

- Open a loop in the first line and close it late.
- Seed a smaller loop in the middle (a re-hook).
- Land something new every few seconds.
- Put the biggest payoff near the end, and end where a replay makes sense.
- Keep every line short enough to read at a glance between takes.
- A talking reel runs as long as its loop holds: a grow reel usually 45 to 100 seconds, a convert reel as short
  as the teaching allows. Shorter is fine when the loop closes sooner: a confessional stops when its question
  lands.

## The ask

- One ask, after the payoff. On a confessional, the payoff is the open question: the story ends there, and the
  ask, if there is one, is one short line after it.
- Curiosity before any keyword. Never lead with the keyword.
- Tie a save to a specific future moment: "save this for the next time...".
- Write a send on purpose: "send this to the friend who...".

## Steal the shape, never the story

When a reel works, keep its **format** (hook type, structure, pacing, the ask, what's on screen) and bring your
own **concept** (topic, examples, story, proof). The test: if the original creator saw yours, they'd recognize
the format but not the idea.

## Formats (what the script needs)

| Engine format | What the script needs |
|---|---|
| Yap | word for word, beats, edit notes |
| Voiceover | the voice track, plus a shot list of the b-roll that proves each line |
| B-roll reels | one hook block per reel (who it's for, then the line), 5 to 8 seconds each, one feeling |
| Animation | a scene list, one idea per scene |

## Test one change at a time

Change one thing between two versions: the hook, the first frame, the audio or the caption. Then the numbers can
say which change did it.

## Outliers

Score each reel against the median plays for its own format (short reels and talking reels separately). A 50K
talking reel beside a 9K talking median is a 5x outlier. The same 50K beside an 18K short-reel median is under
3x.

## Just starting out

A creator with a few reels, no offer and no numbers yet has everything the method needs. It just runs in a
different order.

- **Reach and grow first.** Convert reels wait until there's something to offer. Until then, the ask is to
  follow for the next part, save it, or send it to a friend.
- **Your story is your proof.** What you've lived, tried and learned, and that you're figuring it out in real
  time. Numbers come later. Never borrow one or make one up.
- **Your data starts with you.** Your own moments, and the questions friends ask you, are the first hooks.
  After about ten reels, `what's working for me` starts to show real patterns.
- **Post to learn.** Test one change at a time, so every reel teaches you something.
