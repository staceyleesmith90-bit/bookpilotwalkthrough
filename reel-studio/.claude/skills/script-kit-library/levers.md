# The ten levers

What stops a thumb is human wiring, not luck. Every hook pulls one or more of these. The strongest lines stack
two or three, in this order: something that says it's about them, a label that makes them feel seen, then stakes
that make them stay.

| Lever | The rule | The science, in one line | Meg's example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Name a specific unknown they almost know, and hold the answer | People need to close a gap they can see (Loewenstein) | "Why does nobody talk about the career crash-out that hits in the middle of maternity leave?" |
| Open loop | Start something in line one and finish it near the end | Unfinished things stay louder in the mind (Zeigarnik) | "I have an impossible puzzle to solve." |
| Pattern interrupt | Break the feed's rhythm in the first second | The brain can't help turning toward something new | "And now Claude is editing my reels. Are you kidding me?" (the reel opens on its payoff) |
| Specificity | An exact number, an exact time, or a named moment | Concrete details feel truer and stick longer | "Moms at 2am: thinking... how do I re-start my career and make money without actually leaving my baby." |
| Self-relevance | Put the viewer's own situation in sentence one | Your own name cuts through a noisy room | "I had that 2am thought once." |
| High-arousal emotion | Lead with a feeling that wakes the body: funny, awe, surprise, fair-minded anger. Never manufactured urgency or fear of missing out | Activation, not positivity, drives sharing (Berger and Milkman) | "When the baby makes the decision for you." |
| Loss | Name what's quietly slipping away, and own it as yours | Losses hit about twice as hard as gains (Kahneman and Tversky) | "Somewhere along the way she stopped building something that was hers, and I miss that." |
| Identity | Name the group out loud so sharing it is a badge | People stop for content about who they are (Tajfel and Turner) | "Looking for my people." |
| Social proof | Show that people like them already care | When unsure, people follow similar others (Cialdini) | "Apparently a lot of moms are searching for this." |
| Naming the unnamed | Hand them a short label for a feeling they've never worded | Naming a feeling calms it and makes it shareable (Lieberman) | "We're a new category of moms. We just don't have a name yet." |

Meg's examples show the lever. They are never templates to copy word for word. Write the buyer's own version.

## The lever-to-edit map

Each lever has a script move and an engine edit that makes it land. Edit notes always use the engine's own words
(see `edit-words.md`).

| Lever | Script move | Engine edit |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity gap | Name a specific unknown and hold the answer | Hook block asks the question; the answer lands late as a breakaway card |
| Open loop | Start something in line one, close it near the end | The hook asks the question and stays up the whole reel |
| Pattern interrupt | Break the rhythm in the first second | Zoom-in opener, a punch-in, or a b-roll cutaway in the first beat |
| Specificity | An exact number or a named moment | Count-up on the number |
| Self-relevance | The viewer's own situation in sentence one | Hook block that names them and their moment |
| High-arousal emotion | Lead with the feeling that wakes them up | Matched sound effects and a punch-in on the beat |
| Loss | Frame what they're already losing | Thought bubble with the unsaid cost |
| Identity | Name the group out loud | The category label as the hook's small top line |
| Social proof | Show that people like them already care | B-roll cutaway to real comments or DMs |
| Naming the unnamed | Hand them a short label | Takeover on the label line |

The register sets how much of this goes on one reel. A confessional stays lean: the hook block, one held word,
captions. A teaching reel gets the full set.

## Labels

The lines people share are often labels: a short name for a feeling they have but never put into words. A good
label is two to four words, easy to say, and easy to repeat in a comment. Meg's: "the career crash-out", "the
third option", "the middle".
