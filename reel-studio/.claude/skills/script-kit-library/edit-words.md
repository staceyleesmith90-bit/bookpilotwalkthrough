# Edit words (the engine's own names)

When a script carries edit notes, write them in the engine's own words, so the engine builds exactly that. These
names come from the engine's cheat sheet, "What to Call It" (`product/BUILD-VOCABULARY.md` in the engine folder).
Put a note in brackets at the end of the line it belongs to: `(edit: count-up, $2,000)`. One note per line at
most, and leave most lines bare. Plain words work too, but the names are faster.

## On-screen graphics

| Engine word | What it is | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | the hook block up top that stops the scroll: a small top line and a big line, or one line | the "Read" layer, as `(edit: hook, "night shift moms" / "goodnight at breakfast")`, never a repeat of the spoken line |
| Thought bubble | a small handwritten aside or comment | what they're thinking but not saying, the unsaid cost |
| Held word | one word held on screen for a beat while the voice keeps going | a confessional's one word on screen, the word the reel turns on |
| Count-up | a number that animates on, like $300 ticking up | every exact number they say |
| Breakaway card | a full-screen designed card that breaks away from the footage | a before and after, a list, a summary, or a held answer (teaching reels) |
| B-roll cutaway | a clip that cuts away while the voice keeps going | anything they describe that the viewer should see, like real comments or DMs |
| Text behind you | the hook sitting behind their head, so they pass in front of it | a hook that should feel part of the room |
| Cutout | the creator cut out and placed over a background or card | a before and after, or a reveal |
| Doodles / stickers | the creator's own art, placed and animated | a mark on one word, when they have art to bring |

## Captions

| Engine word | What it is | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Single-word captions | one big word at a time | a line that needs punch |
| Karaoke captions | the whole line on screen, each word lighting up as it's said | a line to read along with |
| Takeover | the words stack and fill the screen for one big beat | the label line, the one people will repeat |

## Motion and sound

| Engine word | What it is | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Zoom-in opener | a slow push-in on the first line | pulling people in on the first second |
| Punch-in | a quick zoom on an accent beat | the accent word, a feeling that should hit |
| Matched sound effects | small sounds that land on the motion | a number landing, a card appearing |
| Music bed | quiet background music under the voice | a reel that needs warmth, or none on a somber one |

## The hook block (one block, three shapes)

The engine puts ONE text block at the top of the frame, in one of three shapes: a one-line hook card, a small top
line over a headline, or a headline over a smaller line. It holds for the opening beats by default, and can stay
up for the whole reel: `(edit: hook, keep it up the whole reel)`.

- A category label belongs IN the hook block, as its small top line: `(edit: hook, top line "career crash-out")`.
- Never ask for a separate label, bar or banner that runs the whole reel on top of the hook. The engine doesn't
  build a second text block unless it's asked for by name, and two blocks compete for the same eyes.
- On a confessional reel, the label names the tension and never resolves it.

## How much goes on one reel

- **Confessional:** lean. The hook, one held word, clean captions. No breakaway cards.
- **Teaching:** the full set. Count-ups, breakaway cards, cutaways, sound on the beats.

## Saying it in the footage

The engine also listens for effect words said while filming: "here's a count-up, five thousand dollars" or
"this is a takeover". It builds the effect on that line, and the line stays in the reel: the engine keeps every
cue line as spoken, it never trims the cue words out. So a spoken cue has to be a line worth keeping. Write it so
it sounds natural said out loud, or use an edit note instead. Edit notes are the main route. Spoken cues are for
the lines where saying it helps the reel anyway.
